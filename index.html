Where Have We Been
2021 · Prisms of the People

Defining power: interactional, multi-faced, constituency-based

  • Power is interactional and dynamic, not a static good — expressed in relationships between political actors that shift as context shifts. To obtain it, organizations must negotiate for it.
  • Power has multiple faces (Lukes, Gaventa): winning the vote, but also a seat at the decision-making table, the terms of the debate, and the narratives beneath it.
  • Resources ≠ power. No linear relationship between people, money, or passion amassed and power exercised. What matters is the prism — the organizational design that refracts a constituency's actions into political power.
"Do you have a community that stands behind what you stand for? If you do, you'll have more power. And if you don't, you won't."
Han, McKenna & Oyakawa · Prisms of the People · ch. 3
2023 · Power Metrics (DPI)

Making power measurable — with the field, not on it

  • The dominant metrics — turnout, contact attempts, head counts — measure individual participation and miss power entirely. Record participation coincided with democratic backsliding: the metrics couldn't see the problem.
  • Co-created with 12 base-building organizations, a framework in three dimensions: base-building (leadership + commitment), collective capacities (solidarity, strategy, structure), and impact & influence (the three faces of power in live arenas).
  • Grounded in cases where power visibly shifted: Ohio (OOC), Minnesota (ISAIAH), Arizona (LUCHA).
The mindset shift: the citizen as democratic agent, not consumer of politics — and measurement that tracks democratic power, not transactions.
Cushman & McKenna · Power Metrics · 2023
2024–25 · Civic Power

Power at scale: what independent political organizations do

  • 26 IPOs, 10 states, an 18-month window. The frame: a power crisis — rising participation without symmetrical civic power.
  • IPOs reached 1,295,275 voters no other program contacted — disproportionately racially diverse, young, and infrequent voters — via 5 million person-to-person conversations.
  • And mobilization wasn't the end: it was a program strong associations wield to influence their power arenas.

2026 · BOB 1.0

The micro-mechanics: how power is built — or spent

  • Person-level records from 20 organizations, 11 states: 38,740 participants, 93,233 attendances — the first look inside the base at scale.
  • 71% come once and never return (93% at rallies). The scarce resource is commitment, built or spent through organizational practice.
  • What holds a newcomer is the social architecture of the first encounter: entering among a standing crew raises return by 12.8 points. Form, not label.
  • Teams — not headcount, not budget — separate the organizations that convert reach into power from those that only mobilize.
Same organization, two mass events: a base assembling (31% woven in) vs. a crowd turning out (9%; the strangers never came back).
McKenna · Many Show Up, Few Return · working paper 2026
