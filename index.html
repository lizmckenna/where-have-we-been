Where Have We Been
Where we started

Power as a prism, not a mirror

  • Prisms of the People (2021): the question is not how many people a movement touches but what its organization lets those people do — independent power constituted through leaders, teams, and strategy.
  • Minnesota Power Metrics (2023): the first serious attempt to measure a state ecosystem's power-building — actors, relationships, faces of power — as a shared instrument, not a one-off study.
  • The bet we made together: power-building can be measured — and measurement can serve the organizing, not just describe it.
Where Have We Been
What we built

From case studies to shared infrastructure

  • 31 data-use agreements across 11 states — organizations opening their internal CRM records to shared research: 38,740 participants, 93,233 attendances in the 2024 cycle alone.
  • 33 prospective interviews — theories of change captured before the election, not rationalized after it.
  • A family of instruments now in live pilots: power mapping (actors + relationships), team effectiveness, and the agency scale.
  • One organization's full 13-year record (78,520 attendances) as the longitudinal deep case.

What we learned · 1
71%

of participants come once and never return

At mass rallies, 93%. The binding constraint on movement capacity is not turnout — it is retention. Most of what mobilization produces evaporates before it becomes anything.

What we learned · 2

The purple line: the thin stratum that matters

Cumulative committed leaders (5+ events, both modes, on a team) across 2024. Organizations with similar turnout differ enormously in the leadership they accumulate — the property we call catchment.
Many Show Up, Few Return (McKenna 2026)
What we learned · 3

What holds a newcomer: who is already in the room

Entering among a standing crew raises 90-day return by 12.8 points — and the gap widens as events grow. A big room with a dense core behaves like an organizing space; a big room of strangers behaves like a rally.
Many Show Up, Few Return (McKenna 2026)
What we learned · 4

Same organization, two mass events

Left: a member convention — 31% of 3,683 attendees woven in through prior small-event ties. Right: a lobby day — 9% woven in; the strangers returned at a rate near zero. A base assembling versus a crowd turning out.

What we learned · 5

Teams are what separate strong organizations from weak ones

Each panel is one organization's membership; colored dots sit inside a detected team. The teamed share — not headcount, not budget — tracks retention, leadership density, and assessed efficacy.
Many Show Up, Few Return (McKenna 2026)
What we learned · 6

Reach without reproduction is an ego network

One large organization's complete who-recruited-whom record: 73 recruiters, 84% pure hubs whose recruits never recruit, twelve reproducers. Recruitment does not cascade unless the organization is built for it.
Many Show Up, Few Return (McKenna 2026)
What we learned · 7

Where the loop closes, capacity compounds

Thirteen years in one organization: the base and the committed-leader stratum accumulate rather than churn. The retained become the leaders who build the containers that hold the next cohort.
Many Show Up, Few Return (McKenna 2026)
What the field taught us

"[The] only number I care about is the purple line… If our most engaged leaders, if that number is going up, that's the juice."

— a lead organizer, recalling his training. The research measured what the most experienced organizers already watched. That convergence is the finding.
